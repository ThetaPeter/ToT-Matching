Assume \(a>0\), \(b>0\), \(c>0\) and \(ab+bc+ca\ge 3\). Without loss of generality we may cyclically permute the variables so that \(a\ge b\ge c>0\).

It is enough to treat the boundary case \(ab+bc+ca=3\), since the strict inequality \(ab+bc+ca>3\) already forces
\[
\sum\frac{a}{\sqrt{a+b}}> \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
Thus we now assume \(ab+bc+ca=3\). In this case the elementary inequality \(s:=a+b+c\ge 3\) holds, with equality if and only if \(a=b=c=1\).

Cauchy’s inequality in Engel form yields
\[
\Bigl(\sum\frac{a}{\sqrt{a+b}}\Bigr)^2\ge\frac{s^2}{\sum(a+b)}.
\]
The right-hand side together with \(s\ge 3\) implies
\[
\sum\frac{a}{\sqrt{a+b}}\ge\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}\ge\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
Equality holds when \(a=b=c=1\). Combining the two cases \(ab+bc+ca=3\) and \(ab+bc+ca>3\) therefore gives the asserted lower bound
\[
\sum\frac{a}{\sqrt{a+b}}\ge\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
