Assume \(x>0\), \(y>0\) and \(z>0\).

The AM–HM inequality on the relevant positive quantities yields
\[
\frac{2(x+y+z)}{3}\ge\frac{3}{\sum 1/(x+y)}.
\]
The displayed relation rearranges at once to
\[
\frac{9}{x+y+z}\le\frac{2}{x+y}+\frac{2}{y+z}+\frac{2}{z+x}.
\]

Equivalently, the same positivity hypothesis gives
\[
\sum\frac{1}{x+y}\ge\frac{9}{2(x+y+z)},
\]
and multiplying through by \(2\) produces
\[
2\sum\frac{1}{x+y}\ge\frac{9}{x+y+z},
\]
which is again the claimed inequality.

Thus
\[
\frac{9}{x+y+z}\le\frac{2}{x+y}+\frac{2}{y+z}+\frac{2}{z+x}.
\]
