### Setup and algebraic identities.
We work throughout with the usual ring operations (in particular, the distributive law). Let \(a\) be an indeterminate (or an element of a commutative ring). By distributivity we have
\[
(a-10)(a+11)=a(a+11)-10(a+11).
\]
Distributing on the first summand yields \(a(a+11)=a^2+11a\), and therefore
\[
(a-10)(a+11)=a^2+11a-10(a+11).
\]
Separately, the identity \(10(a+11)=10a+110\) immediately implies (upon multiplying by \(-1\))
\[
-10(a+11)=-10a-110.
\]
Substituting the latter into the previous expansion gives
\[
(a-10)(a+11)=a^2+11a-10a-110.
\]
The linear terms combine as \(11a-10a=a\), so
\[
a^2+11a-10a-110=a^2+a-110,
\]
and we conclude
\[
(a-10)(a+11)=a^2+a-110.
\]

### Specialization.
The identity just obtained holds in the polynomial ring \(\mathbb{R}[a]\), and likewise holds for every element \(a\) of an arbitrary commutative ring \(R\). In particular, taking \(R=\mathbb{C}\) (or evaluating the identity of \(\mathbb{R}[a]\) at complex numbers) we obtain
\[
\forall\,a\in\mathbb{C},\qquad(a-10)(a+11)=a^2+a-110,
\]
as required.
