The condition that the domain \(D=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:ax^{2}+bx\geq0\}\) equals the range of \(f\) on \(D\) forces \(D\subseteq[0,\infty)\), since the range of \(f\) is contained in \([0,\infty)\). Thus \(D\subseteq[0,\infty)\). Combined with the definition of \(D\), this implies that \(b>0\) (so that \(0\in D\)) and that \(D\) is either \([0,+\infty)\) or a bounded interval \([0,M]\) whose right endpoint is the root \(-b/a\).

The range of \(f\) on \(D\) is likewise an interval of the form \([0,\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x))\) or \([0,f(x_{\mathrm{max}})]\), according to the sign of \(a\) and the location of the vertex.

When \(a=0\) one obtains \(D=[0,+\infty)\) and range \([0,+\infty)\), so equality holds. When \(a<0\), after the harmless scaling \(b=1\), there is exactly one value \(a=-1/4\) for which the range equals \(D\). When \(a>0\) the range is bounded while \(D\) is unbounded, or else the range fails to contain the endpoint \(0\), so no solutions exist.

Hence there are exactly two admissible real values of \(a\).
