Let \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}\). Expanding the product of linear factors gives
\[
(a-b)(a-c)=a^{2}-(b+c)a+bc.
\]
Write \(p:=a^{2}-(b+c)a+cb\). Commutativity of multiplication in \(\mathbb{C}\) yields \(bc=cb\), so
\[
p=(a-b)(a-c).
\]
The right-hand side of the claimed identity is then precisely \(p(a-d)\), and therefore
\[
\text{RHS}=(a-b)(a-c)(a-d).
\]
The left-hand side is the same product (up to the order of the factors), so both sides are identical. The identity therefore holds for all \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}\). This completes the algebraic verification.
