Let the first term of a geometric sequence be \(a\) and the common ratio be \(r\). Then the second term is \(ar=2\) and the fourth term is \(ar^3=6\).

Dividing these two equations yields \(r^2=3\), so \(r=\sqrt{3}\) or \(r=-\sqrt{3}\).

In the first case we obtain \(a=2/\sqrt{3}\). In the second case we obtain \(a=-2\sqrt{3}/3\).

The pair \(a=-2\sqrt{3}/3\), \(r=-\sqrt{3}\) indeed satisfies both \(ar=2\) and \(ar^3=6\), so this value of \(a\) is possible.
