### Setup.
Let \(f\) be a quadratic polynomial, so \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\). We are given the identity \(f(x+3)=3x^2+7x+4\).

### Evaluation at a convenient point.
Substituting \(x=-2\) into the given identity yields \(f(1)\). Explicitly,
\[
f(1)=3(-2)^2+7(-2)+4=12-14+4=2.
\]
On the other hand, the quadratic form of \(f\) immediately gives \(a+b+c=f(1)\). Combining these, we obtain \(a+b+c=2\).

### Independent verification by expanding.
Expanding the left-hand side of the given identity in the quadratic form of \(f\) produces
\[
f(x+3)=ax^2+(6a+b)x+(9a+3b+c).
\]
Equating coefficients with the right-hand side \(3x^2+7x+4\) yields \(a=3\), \(b=-11\), and \(c=10\). Adding these coefficients again gives \(a+b+c=2\).

Thus in all cases \(a+b+c=2\).
