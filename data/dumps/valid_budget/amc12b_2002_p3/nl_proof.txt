By the identity \(n^2-3n+2=(n-1)(n-2)\) (valid for every integer \(n\)), the value at \(n=3\) is \(2\), which is prime. For every positive integer \(n\neq 3\) the same product fails to be prime. (The only positive integer at which one of the factors equals \(1\) is likewise \(n=3\), and the product is then prime.) Hence there is exactly one such positive integer.

Final answer: \(1\)
