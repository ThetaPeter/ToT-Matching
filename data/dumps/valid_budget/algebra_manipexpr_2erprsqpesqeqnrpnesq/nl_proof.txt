### Setup.
Let \(e\) and \(r\) be arbitrary complex numbers. We establish the identity \(2er + e^2 + r^2 = (-r + (-e))^2\) by chaining the given equalities.

### Intermediate identities.
By the given claim \(\mathrm{l4}\), we have
\[
(-r + (-e))^2 = (-e - r)^2.
\]
By the given claim \(\mathrm{l5}\) (which itself rests on \(\mathrm{l3}\) and \(\mathrm{l1}\)), we have
\[
(-e - r)^2 = e^2 + 2er + r^2.
\]
(The same expansion of a square also appears as the given claim \(\mathrm{l6}\), which rests on \(\mathrm{l3}\); likewise \(\mathrm{l2}\) records that \([-(e+r)]^2 = (e+r)^2\).) Combining the two displayed identities therefore yields
\[
(-r + (-e))^2 = e^2 + 2er + r^2.
\]
Finally, the given claim \(\mathrm{l7}\) rearranges the right-hand side by commutativity of addition:
\[
e^2 + 2er + r^2 = 2er + e^2 + r^2.
\]

### Conclusion.
Substituting the last rearrangement into the previous identity produces
\[
2er + e^2 + r^2 = (-r + (-e))^2,
\]
as required.
