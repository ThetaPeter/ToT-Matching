Let \(n=2^a3^b5^c7^d\) where \(a,b,c,d\) are non-negative integers and \(c\ge1\). The prime factorizations of \(5!\) and \(10!\) are known. Consequently the least common multiple of \(n\) with the relevant factorial may be expressed by taking the maximum of corresponding exponents, and the greatest common divisor of \(n\) with the relevant factorial may be expressed by taking the minimum of corresponding exponents. Substituting these two exponent-wise expressions into the given relation between the lcm and five times the gcd produces an equality of exponents for each prime.
