From the given facts, team \(A\) has \(\frac{2}{3}a\) wins and \(\frac{1}{3}a\) losses, while team \(B\) has \(\frac{5}{8}b\) wins and \(\frac{3}{8}b\) losses. Moreover, \(B\) has exactly seven more wins and seven more losses than \(A\). Substituting these relations immediately yields the pair of equations
\[
\frac{5}{8}b=\frac{2}{3}a+7,\qquad\frac{3}{8}b=\frac{1}{3}a+7.
\]
Clearing denominators produces the equivalent integer system
\[
15b=16a+168,\qquad 9b=8a+168.
\]
The same pair of original equations also implies, by subtraction,
\[
\frac14 b=\frac13 a,
\]
and therefore \(b=\frac43 a\). Substituting this linear relation back into either of the cleared equations (or into either of the original fractional equations) forces \(a=42\). Consequently the answer is \(C\).
