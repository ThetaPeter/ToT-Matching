We start from the equation \(7^{x+7}=8^x\). By the addition formula for exponents this is equivalent to \(7^x\cdot7^7=8^x\). Dividing both sides by \(7^x\) yields the equivalent equation \(7^7=(8/7)^x\).

Taking the logarithm base \(8/7\) of both sides therefore gives \(x=\log_{8/7}7^7\). Equivalently, the same identity is obtained by applying \(\log_{8/7}\) to both sides of \(7^7=(8/7)^x\). Direct substitution confirms that this value of \(x\) satisfies the original equation. Moreover the map \(t\mapsto(8/7)^t\) is injective, so the solution is unique.

Hence the given equation may be written in the form \(x=\log_b7^7\) with \(b=8/7\).
