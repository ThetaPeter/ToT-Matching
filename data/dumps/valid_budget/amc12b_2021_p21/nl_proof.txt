The equation under consideration is \(x>0\) together with \(x^{2^{\sqrt{2}}}=2^{2^{x-1}}\). For such \(x\) the given equation is equivalent both to \(x=2^{2^{x-1-\sqrt{2}}}\) and to \(\ln x=2^{x-1-\sqrt{2}}\ln 2\). Substituting the positive number \(\sqrt{2}\) into the former equivalent form shows that \(\sqrt{2}\) itself is a root. The same equivalent form may equally be rewritten as \(\log_2 x=2^{x-1-\sqrt{2}}\). There exists a second root \(r>2\) of this last equation. The larger root moreover satisfies \(r<4\). Consequently the complete set of positive solutions consists of precisely the two numbers \(\sqrt{2}\) and \(r\) with \(2<r<4\). Their sum \(S\) therefore obeys \(2\le S<6\).
